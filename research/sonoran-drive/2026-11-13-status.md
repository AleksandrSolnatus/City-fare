## Where the project has arrived

The central idea changed during playtesting. We began by trying to make a giant worm enjoyable to ride. The more promising direction became driving through its world: a small skimmer crossing an expansive desert inhabited by enormous worms. Escape, observation, route choice and the distant city give the movement a purpose.

Sonoran Drive 003 is a self-contained offline HTML experiment with an approximately 7-metre skimmer, 600-metre NPC worms and an 8 × 8 kilometre landscape. Roads are optional. The city is a destination; bedrock offers refuge within its clearance margin. Roads alone do not grant safety.

The owner's latest visual response favours the broad pale basin, cracked dark ground beneath the skimmer, mountain silhouettes, distant city and distinct environments that feel connected. This is encouraging visual feedback, not a completed gameplay acceptance test.

## What 003 actually changed

- Four ground families: basin/playa, dune sand, gravel fans and washes, and raised bedrock.
- Six authored dune deposits with asymmetric slopes, crescent forms and long curved ridges.
- Ground-normal lighting, a consistent sun, world-fixed surface detail, conforming broken or buried roads, and distant mountains.
- Shared terrain height and material information for visible ground and vehicle contact.
- A sparse, conservative sand-deformation layer with persistent changes and accounted coarse parcels.

The skimmer identity, 002 worm head, entire worm source and encounter states were retained. Horizontal steering, thrust, brake and surge are unchanged from 002. A terrain-specific vertical correction reduced repeated ground clamps while retaining finite lift, gravity and collision support.

The sand model is an editable 1.8-metre skin, not a fully volumetric desert or a grain simulation. Hardpan, gravel and rock are noneditable. Hardpan and gravel remain dangerous worm territory. Authored dunes, arcade locomotion, approximate contact and visual effects must not be presented as validated giant-animal physics.

## Verification already recorded for 003

These are results from the preserved delivery evidence, not tests rerun for this documentation update.

- Final automated suite: **41 passed, 0 failed**. The preceding 40-test suite also passed in a clean reconstruction with byte-identical HTML; the final added parcel-cap regression did not change runtime code.
- The recorded independent source review found no blocker to an unaccepted local playtest. That review does not establish enjoyable controls or complete browser acceptance.
- Numerical basin, dune and all-landform routes arrived in 150.22, 171.77 and 224.65 simulation seconds, with hull remaining at 60%, 100% and 100%.
- Actual Chrome source-module browser routes completed the basin and all-landform routes using ordinary control inputs, without repositioning the car. These were automated routes, not human playtests or full-app HUD benchmarks.
- Maximum recorded sand-accounting residual was approximately **1.38 × 10⁻¹⁰ m³** on the measured routes.
- Recorded floor-contact corrections fell from hundreds with the old vertical controller to zero on the basin route and one on the dune route. This is not a universal no-snap guarantee.
- Eight matched 002/003 regional screenshots, an overhead view, route captures and a 24.97-second canvas video are preserved locally. Worm observer images used actual simulation states but deliberately repositioned the camera vehicle; they are inspection fixtures, not naturally achieved driving moments.

The measured desktop draw rate was about **22 frames/second**, against a nominal 30-frame cap. The 60fps target was not met. The terrain tour recorded one 0.3333-second skipped wall-time interval during recording. Render timing and simulation time are reported separately in the evidence.

## Unresolved problems

Large angular terrain facets, changing camera-relative tessellation, approximate depth/painter sorting and special treatment of skimmer visibility remain. We cannot claim universally stable silhouettes, no shimmer, or perfect intersections.

Actual phone verification is incomplete. A portrait embedded welcome screen loaded, but the native browser surface failed before interaction and capture. The landscape browser pass was not completed. Automated layout checks do not establish physical iPhone/Safari performance or touch quality.

Full hand-driven flat/dune acceptance, a dedicated browser drive-away-and-return scar check, dedicated edge approaches and exhaustive road/rock-boundary views remain outstanding. No numerical result certifies that the game feels good.

## Current artistic direction: a quiet world

The proposed rule is: **the landscape stays still until something powerful enough changes it.**

Preserve the broad quiet flats, large dune shapes, distinct ground colours, city visibility and readable driving. The attraction is the contrast between a calm desert and an enormous animal acting on one part of it.

A possible worm passage would produce an advancing local ridge, a little slipping sand, partial emergence, restrained shedding from armour and a shallow persistent scar. Then the landscape settles again.

This is a design hypothesis. It is not evidence that the current renderer can make such a ridge convincing, nor a requirement to animate every terrain calculation. Constant terrain waves, large particle fountains and repeated screen shake are outside this proposed comparison.
